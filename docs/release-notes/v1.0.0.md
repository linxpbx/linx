The first public release of Linx: a complete phone system for a home or small office, on a server you own.

## Calls

- **Extensions** for everyone, **ring groups** that ring a team all at once or in turn, and **office hours** with holidays.
- **Incoming numbers** set up with a short wizard: who rings, what happens if nobody answers, and what callers get when you're closed.
- **Voicemail** in the web app, and from a desk phone by dialling `*97`, with the message-waiting light on desk phones.
- **Call history** for everyone, with missed calls first and one tap to ring back.
- **Calls in the browser**: a phone in the web app, with presence so you can see who's free.
- **Desk phones and softphones** sign in over an encrypted connection.

## The iPhone and iPad app

- Scan a code to set a phone up, and it signs itself in from then on. No passwords on the phone.
- **It rings when the phone is asleep**, locked, or the app has been closed — on the lock screen, in the car and on a headset, like any other call.
- Calls, voicemail, your team with who's free, a keypad, and `*97`.
- An **iPad looks like an iPad**: a list beside what you're looking at, and a call beside it rather than over it. A folding phone is asked where it folds.
- **Up to four Linx accounts on one phone, all live at once.** No switching: every account rings, and the call says which one it came to.
- **Test this network** in Settings, for when a hotel or a mobile network is the thing getting in the way.

## Video and group calls

- **Video calls** between two people, with the picture going **straight between the two devices** and never through the server.
- **Group calls**: turn a call into a meeting of up to eight people with **Add person**.
- The picture **follows the connection** — it starts quickly, climbs to HD when there's room, and drops back rather than breaking up. Sound always comes first.
- Video works in the browser too, and a video call carries on in a small window over other apps on a phone.

## Phone lines and countries

- Bring your own phone line provider, or connect the office phone system or gateway you already have.
- **Any country**: Linx reads numbers the way they're dialled where you are, and its emergency numbers always work.
- **What phones can call**: local, mobile, free, abroad and premium-rate numbers, each on or off. Calls abroad can go everywhere or **only to the countries you choose**.
- Alerts for every emergency call, the first call to a new country, and unusual calling abroad.

## Calls that get through

- The sound of a **Linx-to-Linx call goes device to device** in high quality, with the server out of the way.
- On a network that **holds UDP back** — some mobile networks, hotel Wi-Fi, China — calls go over an encrypted TLS connection instead, by themselves.
- A call that has quietly died now ends, instead of sitting there.
- **What a phone saw of each call** is kept beside that phone in the web app, along with any crash, so a complaint about one call can actually be looked into.

## Safe by default

- Passkeys or an authenticator app for signing in, and company sign-in (Google, Microsoft and others).
- Sensitive changes ask you to confirm it's you, and every change to where calls go can be undone.
- Calls are encrypted; nothing is sent in the clear.

## Running it

- **One command** installs Linx on a Linux server (Intel/AMD or ARM), then you finish in your browser.
- Certificates are obtained and renewed for you; **backups** and restore are built in.
- **Help** is built in and searchable, and the same guides are on linxpbx.com.
- Runs on a small server: 1 core and 1–2 GB of memory.
