# Linx — video calls that work: one to one, and adding people

*Owner's go-ahead 2026-10-12 ("Do one to one and add people as well"), after build 26's video call between their iPad and iPhone was poor: one-way picture, no sound from the iPad, slow to start, never smooth. ADR-087 and ADR-088 record the decisions; this file is the design and the build order. **Built 2026-10-13: §9.***

## 1. In plain words
- **Calls still ring exactly as they do now:** push, the lock screen, CallKit, Answer, the browser's ring. Desk phones and outside numbers are untouched.
- **Between two Linx users** (the app or the browser, either side), the **picture goes straight from one device to the other**, the way FaceTime and WhatsApp do one-to-one calls. The sound keeps the route it has today, through the phone system, so the voice never depends on the picture.
- **Video on and off mid-call** is a camera switched on or off on that direct link: a second, no message through the phone system, no prompt that comes and goes.
- **The browser does video** from now on, so app ↔ browser video calls work.
- **Add person** in a Linx-to-Linx call turns it into a **group call** on Linx's own video server (LiveKit, ADR-002): everyone in it moves there together, the new person is rung like any call, and each person leaves on their own. Up to **8 people**.
- **Desk phones and outside numbers join group calls in Phase 3** (LiveKit's phone bridge), as planned.

## 2. Why the picture leaves Asterisk (ADR-087)
The 2026-10-12 22:29 call (both phones' Call records, Asterisk's log): the network was fine (iPad direct, 3 ms; iPhone relayed over UDP, 29 ms). What went wrong was the path. **Asterisk bridges video as a plain relay**: it doesn't pass on the messages two WebRTC ends use to agree on quality (loss and bandwidth feedback, "send a fresh picture"), so each phone guessed — choppy, HD made no difference. And adding a camera was a **re-INVITE through Asterisk to each side in turn**: the iPhone's answer and its camera offer arrived together 4.5 s late, Asterisk renegotiated the iPad's side, and the iPad never showed the iPhone's picture. Two months of fixes have been fixes to that path.

A direct WebRTC link between the two devices has none of this: one connection, end-to-end congestion control and keyframe requests, and the picture can be turned on and off locally.

## 3. One to one: the direct picture
### 3.1 The call link (server)
`GET /api/v1/call-link?call=<SIP Call-ID>` — a websocket each Linx device opens. The app opens it **as its call starts ringing** and the browser once the call is answered. It takes what `GET /api/v1/team/live` takes: a browser session (with the `Origin` check) or an app's device token.
- **Who is in the call is Asterisk's word, not the device's.** The control plane asks ARI for the channel whose SIP Call-ID (`protocol_id`) is the one given **and** whose endpoint is one of the asking person's own devices; anything else is refused. The other side is that channel's `BRIDGEPEER`, and its endpoint names its device. A device can only ever be linked to the person it is actually talking to.
- **A call still ringing is held, not refused** (2026-10-14). If the device's own channel is there but not yet answered, the websocket is accepted and the server sends `{"type":"wait"}` every 10 s. It is woken by Asterisk's own call events, with no polling, and pairs the link the moment the call is answered. It closes the link if the call ends unanswered, and after 3 minutes at most. The owner's iPhone on a bad 5G link took 9 s to open a new connection after the answer, and then 18 s to receive one more message. A link opened while ringing is already there. A call ID that isn't the device's own is still refused at once (404).
- **On the app's own phone line** (2026-10-14). When the server says so on the `/sip` upgrade (`Linx-Call-Links: 1`), the app carries its call links inside the phone line it already has open, as side messages that start with `{` (no SIP message does): `{"call":ID,"open":true}`, `{"call":ID,"msg":M}` and `{"call":ID,"close":true}` from the phone, and `{"call":ID,"msg":M}` and `{"call":ID,"closed":"why"}` from Linx, where M is exactly what the call link's websocket carries. They never reach Asterisk. The pairing, the ringing hold and the checks are the same; the line is the phone's identity and is rechecked by the relay. Why: on the owner's iPhone on Etisalat 5G every newly opened connection was slow for its first 20–30 s, while the line, open for a while, answered in a tenth of a second. A call link of its own took 17–24 s a message, and the picture came 45 s after the answer. The browser, and an app talking to an older server, keep the websocket of its own.
- **The picture is set up while the call rings** (2026-10-14, build 35, `internal/calllink/early.go`). An app on its phone line opens its link with `"early":true`. While the call rings, Linx asks Asterisk for the call's `linkedid`. If it is the device's own channel, the device made the call: it is sent `{"type":"early","session":S,"offer":true}` and offers at once, with its camera on a video call. If not, the device is being rung for the channel `linkedid` names. It is sent `{"type":"early","session":S}` and then the caller's offer and routes as they come, kept for devices that come later (32 messages, 64 KB). It holds the offer and sends nothing back until its person answers. Then its answer goes up its line right behind the 200 OK. Linx keeps what it can't pass on yet, and gives it to the caller once the two are paired. The pairing carries on from the exchange (`"early":true`, the same session), so nothing is said twice. Why: after the answer, the old way took a message down the answering phone's line, one up and one down again. On the owner's iPhone, just woken on 5G, the messages coming down took 5–7 s each (call of 2026-10-14 20:53), and the pictures came 9 and 11 s after the answer. Now only the answer goes, and it goes up. An app that doesn't say `early`, or a browser, is paired afresh as before.
- **The answer and the call's 200 OK go in one message** (build 35). Where the line says `Linx-Call-Links-SIP: 1`, the app answers a call by sending `{"call":ID,"msg":<picture answer>,"sip":"<200 OK>"}`. The relay sends the SIP part to Asterisk first, with exactly the checks a SIP message sent on its own gets, then hands the rest to the call link. Sent one after the other, the phone's network held the second until the first was acknowledged (Nagle's rule; Apple's websocket can't turn it off). On a line whose acknowledgements come back late, that held the picture's answer 3 s on the bench. An offer that reaches the rung phone after its person answered is answered at once with the camera in it, as a held one is. Answered without the camera, the camera came after in a second round that crossed the other side's.
- **The bench's model of the owner's call** (`newLine`): the rung phone starts after the caller, so its line is just open, and what Linx sends on that line is 3 s late from the ringing until 10 s after the answer. With the old setup (`LINX_BENCH_FLAGS=no-early`) both pictures came 7.3 s after the answer, with the same "both offered" crossing as the owner's report. Now they come in 1.2–1.3 s.
- **Picture size follows the room** (build 35). The app holds the size it asks for, so it brings it down itself: straight to the step whose most is no more than twice the link's room, after two readings. It goes back up a step after three readings with room for about two thirds of the next step's most. Before, only a link under 150 kbit/s moved it, and HD stayed at 720p on 160–400 kbit/s, stopping and starting.
- **Both ends connected = paired.** Each is told `{"type":"peer","video":true,"polite":…}`; if the other side is not a Linx device (a desk phone, a trunk) it is told `video:false` and the video button stays off, as today.
- **Then it only relays:** `{"type":"signal",…}` from one end is passed to the other unchanged (offers, answers, ICE candidates). At most 64 KB a message and 50 a second. When either end goes, the other is told `peer-gone`. Channels are re-checked every 15 s (the `/sip` relay's rule), so a link never outlives its call.
- In-memory, one hub in the control plane; nothing stored.

### 3.2 The direct link (app and browser)
A second `RTCPeerConnection` beside the call's own, **for the picture only**, made when the link pairs and kept for the whole call, so turning video on is adding a track, not a new call. *Perfect negotiation* (the WebRTC pattern for two sides that may both offer: `polite` decides who gives way). ICE through Linx's own TURN with the credentials the call already has.
- **coturn** may now relay to its own relay address as well as Asterisk's (two relayed devices reach each other through it), and to LiveKit's (§4). Every other peer stays refused; TURN's own permissions stop anyone sending to an allocation that didn't ask for them.
- **Quality**: **AV1 first** between two apps (owner, 2026-10-15, build 37): the same picture in about two thirds of H.264's data, so the three small steps' caps are ⅔ of H.264's and a thin link carries a bigger picture; 540p and 720p keep H.264's full caps (build 40: a phone encodes AV1 in its fastest mode, and 720p at ⅔ came out full size but soft). **On mobile data the picture starts at 480p** and grows as the link shows room (**HD** skips the wait): an iPhone on a weak operator's upload choked its own sound with a 540p start. Readings in the report also give kbit/s and the average quantiser (qp, lower is sharper) each way. AV1 is the phone's processor's work (libaom in Google's WebRTC; dav1d decodes), so a phone whose encoder is held back by its processor for three readings running offers again without AV1 and the call goes on in H.264 (hardware). A side without AV1 (a browser lacking it) meets the app on H.264 as before. Reports say which codec and how many ms a frame takes. Before build 37: H.264 first on the app (hardware), WebRTC's own congestion control, a cap per the route (ADR-081's ceilings), and the camera off by itself when there isn't room for voice. ADR-081's ladder stays as the cap; WebRTC does the adapting.
- **Lip sync**: sound and picture take different paths, so they are not tied together; both are short on the same network. Measured and recorded in `docs/RESOURCES.md`; if it's noticeable, the sound moves onto the direct link too (§8).

### 3.3 Ringing as a video call (replaces ADR-086 (1))
The INVITE carries **no video at all**; the app and the browser send `X-Linx-Video: yes` on a video call. The dialplan carries it to every leg it rings and to the wake push (`Video:` on `LinxWake`), so CallKit and the browser show "Video call" while it rings, as now. Migration 0052 takes `h264,vp8` and the video stream off the app's and the browser's endpoints (ADR-086 (3)'s bundling stays): Asterisk carries sound only. An older app that still offers a picture gets the stream refused and the call goes on as sound.

**On mobile data (build 36, 2026-10-15)** a phone sends its picture through the relay alone (`MobileData`, `iceTransportPolicy = relay` for the picture's connection only). On the owner's 5G, a direct route came and went all call long, and the picture froze each time. A phone on Wi-Fi still goes direct.

## 4. Adding people: group calls on LiveKit (ADR-088)
### 4.1 What happens
1. In a Linx-to-Linx call, either person presses **Add person** and picks someone from the Team list.
2. The control plane makes a **room**, gives both people a LiveKit token over the call link (`{"type":"move",…}`), and **rings the new person** through Asterisk (ARI originate into a small `linx-room-invite` context that rings their devices with the wake push, as any call), with `X-Linx-Room` on the INVITE.
3. Both join the room, their pictures and sound go there, and they **end their SIP call** (BYE) — the app keeps its CallKit call; only what carries it changed. Each person now leaves on their own.
4. The new person answers; their device sees `X-Linx-Room`, asks for its token over the call link (the server knows that channel carries that room), joins, and ends its SIP leg.
5. Anyone in the room can **Add person** again, up to 8. The room closes when the last person leaves.

### 4.2 Who may join a room
A token is minted only for a device whose own live channel carries that room (the invite) or that was in the call that became the room. Tokens are HS256 with LiveKit's key (a Docker secret the installer generates), one room, publish and subscribe, five-minute life (LiveKit keeps a joined participant in). Room names are random. **Presence**: the control plane counts room members as "on a call" in the Team list.

### 4.3 LiveKit on the server
- `linx-livekit`: the pinned upstream image (Apache-2.0, Go), non-root, read-only, `mem_limit` sized from measurement. It runs all the time: starting it only for a room would need Docker control the control plane must never have (security rule), so it is kept small instead (measured idle and in a 3-person call, `docs/RESOURCES.md`).
- **Signalling** through the control plane at `/livekit/` (a websocket proxy to `linx-livekit:7880` on `linx-private`), so **no new hostname, port or front-door change**.
- **Media**: one UDP port (`7882`, ICE/UDP mux) published on the LAN address only, like Asterisk's audio, and reached through coturn on `linx-media` from anywhere else. LiveKit's own TURN is off; clients use Linx's.
- **The phones**: the browser uses `livekit-client` (Apache-2.0), loaded only when a group call starts. The app speaks LiveKit's signalling itself on the WebRTC it already has (one video engine, one audio session under CallKit), with a small protobuf reader/writer for the messages it uses.

## 5. Security (STRIDE rows go into `docs/THREAT_MODEL.md` with the build)
- The call link trusts Asterisk's channels, not what a device says; relayed messages are size- and rate-limited and never interpreted by the server.
- A direct link exposes a device's addresses to the other person in the call — the same as any WebRTC call app; relay-only is still possible and is what the app falls back to.
- While a video call rings, the devices being rung receive the caller's offer and so the caller's addresses (the caller chose to call them). A device being rung sends nothing until its person answers, so a caller learns nothing about a device that doesn't answer. Which devices are being rung is Asterisk's `linkedid`, never the device's word.
- coturn's peer list grows by two addresses, both inside the server.
- LiveKit's API is reachable only on `linx-private`; tokens are minted only by the control plane; media is DTLS-SRTP. Group calls are **not end-to-end encrypted** (as calls through Asterisk aren't); E2EE stays Phase 3's meetings.

## 6. Resources
Nothing new for a 1:1 call: the picture no longer passes through Asterisk at all, and directly connected devices cost the server nothing. LiveKit is the one new service; its idle and busy cost, and the data a minute of a group call uses, are measured and written into `docs/RESOURCES.md` before release.

## 7. Build order (one release at the end: one server update, one app build)
1. **Server, 1:1:** the video header through the dialplan and the push, migration 0052, the call link and its ARI checks, coturn's peers. Call suite: pairing proved on the real Asterisk.
2. **Browser, 1:1:** the direct picture, the **Video call** button working, mid-call video on/off. Browser suite: two browsers on a video call.
3. **App, 1:1:** the direct picture replacing video through SIP; the old SIP-video code removed.
4. **Server, groups:** `linx-livekit`, its secret and config, the `/livekit/` proxy, tokens, Add person (originate, the invite context), presence.
5. **Browser, groups:** the group call screen.
6. **App, groups:** LiveKit signalling, the group call screen (iPhone, iPad).
7. **Finish:** help guides, `docs/RESOURCES.md`, `docs/THREAT_MODEL.md`, `docs/HISTORY.md`, the release.

- The 4.5 s between the iPhone sending its answer and Asterisk receiving it on the 22:29 call: looked at in step 3.

## 9. As built (2026-10-13)

- **Server:** `internal/calllink` (the hub, and the ARI resolver: `protocol_id`, `BRIDGEPEER`, `LINXROOM`, the originate), `services/control-plane/calllink.go`, `groupcall.go` (`POST /api/v1/group-calls`, `/join`, `/{room}/people`) and `livekit.go` (the `/livekit/` websocket relay, `/rtc` and `/rtc/validate` only: a plain reverse proxy would have been cut by the server's 30-second timeouts); `internal/livekit` (configuration, HS256 tokens, `ListParticipants` over Twirp); `services/livekit-entrypoint` and `deploy/docker/livekit.Dockerfile` (the official v1.13.9 binary on distroless as nonroot; LiveKit's key reaches it through the environment because it refuses a key file that isn't 0600). Migration 0052; the dialplan's `linx-headers` and `linx-room-invite`; coturn's three peers; `linx doctor`, System → Status, CI and the release workflow know `linx-livekit`. `Permissions-Policy` now allows this origin's camera.
- **LiveKit's network:** it advertises **one address, its own on `linx-media`**, and publishes no port at all. Every device reaches it through coturn, over UDP where the network allows and TLS 443 where it doesn't — the same path browsers have always used to reach Asterisk from outside. LiveKit's own TURN and outside-address lookups are off.
- **Browser:** `web/src/phone/picture.ts` (the direct picture, perfect negotiation), `web/src/phone/group.ts` (`livekit-client`, loaded only when a group call starts: simulcast, dynacast, adaptive stream), the call panel's pictures, camera button, **Add person** and group grid; a camera choice in Settings.
- **App:** `DirectPicture` (the direct picture and `CallLinkSocket`), `LiveKitWire` (the protobuf messages, by hand from LiveKit's `livekit_rtc.proto`, protocol 12) and `GroupCall` (publisher and subscriber connections on the call's own WebRTC factory, one camera layer at 540p), `GroupCallView` and `AddPersonSheet`. SIP's video code, the quality ladder and the frame watch are gone.
- **Proved here:** the call suite (the call link finds each device's own leg on the real Asterisk); the coturn suite (one device's relay reaches another's); the browser suite (a browser video call with the cameras going off and on, a video call to a desk phone falling back to sound, a three-person group call with Add person, an invitation, everyone seeing everyone, and people leaving one by one); the app's tests (SIP never carries a picture; a group call survives the phone call underneath it) and `LiveKitLiveTests` against a real LiveKit server.
- **Not proved until the owner's phones:** the app's picture and group call over real networks — the browser and the app speak the same call link and LiveKit, but only a real iPhone and iPad show the camera, the audio session under CallKit and 5G together.
- **Left for later:** presence for people in a group call (they show as on a call only while the phone call underneath lasts), simulcast from the app, and desk phones in group calls (Phase 3, `livekit-sip`).

## 10. The phone bench, and what it found (2026-10-13)

Builds 27–29 each failed on the owner's two devices on 5G (no picture either way; a call that dropped by itself; a slow start), and each fix was a guess. From now on **no app build or server update goes to the owner until `make test-phones` passes** (owner, 2026-10-13: "never do anything unless you are 100% it works").

**The bench** (`internal/browsertest/phonebench_docker_test.go`, `ios/tools/bench.sh`, `ios/Linx/Core/Bench.swift`): the whole stack in Docker behind `linx-sni`, the app in two simulators that set themselves up from a setup link, call each other as a **video call**, answer and hang up with nobody touching them (launch arguments, debug builds only). The domain is `linx.127.0.0.1.nip.io`, so the simulators use real names and TLS against this Mac's published ports; a simulator has no camera, so `Camera` sends a moving test picture there. A call passes only if both phones' own records say: connected, sound arrived both ways, **the other side's picture arrived** (frame counts), and the call ended because it was hung up. Scenarios:

| Scenario | How |
|---|---|
| Mobile network | every route through the relay (`iceTransportPolicy = relay`) |
| China | no UDP: the relay over TLS on 443 only |
| China, connection cut mid-call | every connection to port 443 reset and refused for 4 s, 15 s into the call (`iptables` in `linx-sni`'s namespace) |
| Poor mobile link | 300 ms round trip, 2% loss, on what Linx sends the phones (`tc netem`, the phone-facing side only) |
| China on a poor link | the same, 1% loss, TLS only |

CallKit is not on the bench: a simulator's own call service ends an app's outgoing CallKit call about a second in (`callservicesd` asks for the `CXEndCallAction` itself), so the bench takes the in-app path China uses (ADR-078). CallKit stays a check on a real phone.

**What it found, all fixed and proved by it:**
1. **No picture on any app-to-app video call.** Both cameras go on when a video call is answered, so both sides offer at once. The polite side must roll its own offer back first; a browser does that by itself, **WebRTC for iOS does not**, so their offer was refused and neither picture ever came. Browser-to-browser always worked, which is why the browser suite never caught it. Now: explicit rollback; no offer of its own while theirs is being used; its camera put into *their* offer's slot (a browser reuses its slot by itself, iOS doesn't); and the polite side waits 1.5 s for the other's offer before offering its own, which saves the collision altogether.
2. **A call hung up by itself on a slow link.** Asterisk resends its 200 OK until the phone's ACK arrives; the app took the copy for a new answer, failed to apply it, and sent BYE. A repeated answer is now acknowledged again and nothing else.
3. **A dropped connection ended the call.** The line to Linx (the `/sip` websocket) closing — a phone moving between networks — ended the call at once, though sound and picture travel elsewhere. Now the call is **held for 30 s**, the phone reconnects every second, and on its new line sends an **UPDATE** (RFC 3311, a target refresh) so Asterisk sends the call's requests down the new connection (`SIPUserAgent.reattach`, `refreshTheCall`).
4. **Sound never came back after a cut.** The sound's ICE state was watched and nothing acted on it. A route that fails, or drops for 3 s, is now looked for again (an ICE restart sent as a re-INVITE) once the line is back, retried until there is a network to offer, and a re-INVITE lost with a dropped connection is given up after 8 s.
5. **The picture's link after a reconnect.** Each pairing now has a number (`session` on `peer`, `internal/calllink`); both ends stamp what they send with it and drop what belongs to an earlier pairing (an old offer landing on the new connection jammed it). A rebuilt link turns the camera back on.
6. **China on a slow link.** The relay over TLS takes more than 3 s to set up on a 300 ms link, and an offer without it had nowhere for the sound to go: the phone now waits up to 10 s for its first route. Over TCP or TLS the picture is kept at **360p (350 kbit/s)** and the other side is told to do the same (`slowRoute`): 540p built up more than a second of delay there. A thin link steps the picture down (360p, then 240p) and back up when there is room, and only a link too thin even for 240p switches the camera off.
7. The picture's relay route is prepared while the link opens, not after it: on a clean network the picture now arrives about 2 s after the answer, about 8 s on a 300 ms / 2% link.

Not on the bench, and so still for a real phone: CallKit, a PushKit wake, a real camera and microphone, and 5G itself.
