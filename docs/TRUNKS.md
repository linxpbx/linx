# Linx — Phone lines to the outside world (Phase 1D)

Status: **approved by the owner 2026-09-26** (ADR-043 to ADR-048), with two changes to the drafted defaults recorded in §11: the demo uses the UCM6304's own phone lines, and 901 joins the UAE's always-allowed numbers. Steps 1 (numbering), 2 (data and API), 3 (Asterisk trunks), 4 (probe, CLI, doctor, alerts) and 5 (WireGuard) are built (§4, §5, §7, §9, §10 "As built").
Fourth slice of Phase 1 (`docs/ROADMAP.md`). Until now Linx only calls itself: extensions, desk phones and browsers. This slice connects it to real phone numbers, so people can call mobiles and landlines, and outside callers can reach them. The admin portal screens for all of this come in 1E; this slice gives the engine, the API, a guided `linx trunk add` command and `linx doctor` checks.

## 1. In plain words
- **A trunk is a phone line from a provider** (a company that sells phone numbers and calls over the internet), or a link to **another phone system** you already own, like the Grandstream UCM6304 with the landlines plugged into it. One trunk can carry many calls at once.
- **Encrypted first, always tried first.** Linx connects to the provider over TLS with encrypted audio (SRTP). Only if the provider can't do that does Linx offer an unencrypted connection, and only after you confirm a plain warning ("Calls to and from this provider can be listened to on the way"), ADR-023. Trunks through a WireGuard tunnel are encrypted by the tunnel and get no warning (ADR-024).
- **Nothing new is opened to the internet** for the usual kind of trunk (Linx signs in to the provider, like a phone does). A provider that instead sends calls from fixed addresses gets a door opened for exactly those addresses, nobody else.
- **Your phone numbers ("DIDs") ring someone.** Each number points to an extension (ring groups, menus and office hours come with the routing wizards later in Phase 1).
- **Outgoing calls are dialled the way you'd dial on a mobile.** Choose your country once. Linx understands local, mobile, national, international, toll-free and premium numbers from Google's open numbering database, and decides who may call what. International calls are **off** until you turn them on; premium numbers are blocked; **emergency numbers always work** (if your provider carries them).
- **Protection against phone fraud** (criminals who break into phone systems to call expensive numbers): calls from a trunk can never go back out through a trunk, each extension has a limit on outside calls at once, and you get an alert on unusual international calling and on every emergency call.
- **A test before you save.** `sudo linx trunk add` asks plain questions (or starts from a provider template), checks the connection (name, certificate, encryption, sign-in), and only then saves. `linx route test 0501234567 --from 101` shows exactly what would happen to a call, without making it.

## 2. What runs
| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| Asterisk (existing) | Gains the trunks: a TLS client transport, a plain one used only for ADR-023 trunks, trunk contexts, and outbound decisions read from the database (§5). Transcodes Opus (browsers) ↔ G.711 (providers) with the codec built in 1C. |
| Control plane (existing) | Stores trunks (passwords sealed, ADR-030), renders them for Asterisk (ADR-043), probes them, watches their state over ARI, fills the numbering tables (ADR-044), raises alerts and events. |
| `linx-wireguard` (new, small) | Brings up WireGuard tunnels for trunks that use one (ADR-046). The only container with `NET_ADMIN`. |
| `linx-firewall-sync` (new, host systemd timer) | Keeps the host firewall's per-trunk address sets in step with the trunks (ADR-047). Only needed for trunks that call in from fixed addresses. |

## 3. Kinds of trunk
| Kind (plain name) | How calls come in | Firewall | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"Linx signs in to the provider"** (registration) | Over the connection Linx opened | nothing opened | ✅ recommended; works with a changing home address |
| **"The provider sends calls to Linx"** (IP-authenticated) | Provider connects to `sip.<domain>:5061` (TLS) from its published addresses | 5061/tcp (and the audio ports) opened **only to those addresses**; the router forwards 5061. 5060 only for ADR-023 trunks, same addresses only | Warned: needs a fixed home address or DDNS the provider accepts |
| **"Another phone system on my network"** (LAN peer: UCM6304, another Linx) | Either side calls the other | LAN only, nothing on the internet | For a parallel run: send `2xx` to the UCM while people move over |
| Any of the above **"through a WireGuard tunnel"** | Inside the tunnel | nothing on the internet | For providers that offer a VPN; no ADR-023 warning |

Each trunk has: name, kind, provider address (host, port; SRV used when the provider publishes it), login (for registration), transport (TLS or, with the ADR-023 confirmation, TCP/UDP), media encryption (SRTP or, confirmed, none), certificate trust (§6), codecs (default `alaw,ulaw` for the country; G.722 if offered), how numbers are sent (`+971…`, `00971…` or `04…`), caller ID rules (§5), its DIDs, maximum calls at once, **Connection** (Internet or a WireGuard profile, ADR-024), and whether it's the primary or backup line for outgoing calls.

**Provider templates** (a small, versioned YAML catalogue in the image, easy to add to): Telnyx, VoIP.ms, Twilio Elastic SIP Trunking, Grandstream UCM, "Another Linx", Generic. A template fills in addresses, transport, number format and codecs; the probe still checks everything.

## 4. How trunks reach Asterisk (ADR-043)
- Devices stay in realtime views (ADR-032). **Trunks don't**: a provider password must be usable by Asterisk (it signs in to the provider with it), so it can't be a one-way hash like device logins, and putting it in a view would leave it readable in the database. Instead the control plane opens the sealed secret and **renders `pjsip_trunks.conf`** (endpoints, auths, registrations, `identify` rules, the SIP ACL) into a memory-only volume shared only with Asterisk (like the 1C `sipws-certs` volume: tmpfs, group-readable by Asterisk only), then asks Asterisk over ARI to reload `res_pjsip` (`PUT /asterisk/modules/res_pjsip.so`). 1B showed that reload keeps registrations and live calls. Outbound registrations can't be added through realtime without a reload anyway.
- At start, Asterisk's entrypoint waits for the file (as it waits for the websocket certificate); if the control plane is down, the last file stays in place and calls keep working.
- **The SIP ACL moves into that file**: PJSIP applies every ACL object separately (a request must pass all), so LAN networks, the browser network and trunk addresses must be one list. `internal/asteriskconf` keeps rendering the device-only ACL until the first trunk file arrives.
- Each trunk's inbound calls land in **`linx-from-trunk`**, which can reach only that trunk's DIDs (then an extension), never `linx-outbound`. No trunk-to-trunk, no dialling out from an inbound call: the classic open-relay fraud can't happen by construction (tested).
- *As built (step 3, 2026-09-26):* `internal/trunkconf` renders `pjsip_trunks.conf` + `pinned-ca.pem` (per enabled trunk `trunk-<uuid>`: aor with keep-alive checks every 60 s, auth when it has a login, endpoint in `linx-from-trunk` with `identify_by=ip` so a From header can never name a trunk, registration with `line=yes` for "Linx signs in" trunks, `identify` by resolved address for the others). The control plane (`trunkconf.Renderer`: at start, on every trunk create/change/delete via `trunk.Service.OnChange`, and every minute for DNS changes; a host whose DNS fails keeps its last addresses) writes them into the tmpfs volume `trunks` (compose; uid 65532, group 101, `2750`), only when they change, atomically. **Reload is done by Asterisk's entrypoint, not over ARI** (changed from the design: ARI stays read-only): it waits up to 2 minutes for the file at start, then checks both files every 2 s and runs `module reload res_pjsip.so` on a change; registrations, phones and calls in progress survive (tested). **The ACL:** `pjsip.conf` keeps the phones' `[phone-networks]` and ends with `#tryinclude` of the trunk file, which adds every trunk's addresses with `[phone-networks](+)` (one ACL object; registration providers too, since their calls arrive from their own address). Dialplan (`internal/asteriskconf`): phones' short numbers go through `linx-local` (an extension, else out); everything else through `linx-outbound`; trunks' calls through `linx-from-trunk` → `linx-trunk-call` (DID from the request, else the `To` header, via `asterisk.linx_inbound`) → `linx-ring`. A unit test walks the dialplan's `Goto`s from `linx-from-trunk` and fails if `linx-outbound` is reachable. Inbound caller names are filtered to letters, digits, space and `.` (40 characters), numbers to digits and `+` (20). Image gains `res_pjsip_outbound_registration`, its digest authenticator, `func_cut/strings/groupcount` and `ca-certificates`.

## 5. Numbers and routing (ADR-044)
- **Country** (default United Arab Emirates, +971) is chosen once. The control plane uses **`github.com/nyaruka/phonenumbers`** (MIT, a Go port of Google's libphonenumber, whose data is updated monthly) to write the country's numbering rules into tables: national and international prefixes, and for each call type (local/fixed, mobile, toll-free, premium, shared-cost, international) the pattern libphonenumber uses. Emergency numbers come from libphonenumber's short-number data, checked against a small built-in list per country (UAE: 999 police, 998 ambulance, 997 fire, 112, and 901 police non-emergency, owner decision).
- **The decision happens in the database**, not in the control plane: a SQL function `asterisk.linx_route_outbound(caller, dialled)` normalises the number to E.164, classifies it with those patterns (PostgreSQL's regular expressions should accept libphonenumber's; step 1 proves it or translates them), checks the caller's permission, and returns the trunk(s) to try and the number in each trunk's format. The dialplan calls it through `func_odbc`, exactly like ring targets today. So outgoing calls, like internal ones, **keep working while the control plane restarts** (ADR-034's rule). A Docker test compares the SQL function with the Go library on a few thousand numbers per supported country.
- **What people dial:** a number as they'd dial it on a mobile (`050 123 4567`, `04 123 4567`, `+44 20…`, `0044 20…`). No "9 for an outside line". Extension numbers (2–6 digits) can't start with the national or international prefix (`0` in the UAE) or equal an emergency or short service number; creating one is refused, and choosing a country flags any existing clash.
- **Who can call what:** permission levels, default "Staff: local, mobile, national, toll-free" and "Managers: + international", assigned per extension (per person and group when 1E brings them). Premium is off for everyone unless turned on per level; emergency is always on and can't be turned off.
- **Which line:** primary trunk, then backup if the primary is down, busy (its call limit reached) or answers with a failure that means "try elsewhere" (e.g. 503), never after the called person answers or rejects.
- **Caller ID** (what people you call see): per trunk a main number; per extension its own DID if it has one; "withheld" per level if the provider supports it. Sent in `From` and `P-Asserted-Identity` as the trunk template says. Inbound caller names are untrusted text: stripped of control characters and quotes and length-limited before they reach the dialplan, the API, webhooks or the web page.
- **Inbound:** each DID → an extension. A call to a DID nobody has set up hears "not in use". A DID on no trunk can't be claimed twice.
- *As built (step 1, 2026-09-26):* `internal/numbering` (tables from `nyaruka/phonenumbers` v1.8.1, `Classify` = the library's own answer, plain-words text), migration `0016_numbering.sql` (`pbx_setting.country` default `AE`; tables `numbering_region/desc/short/always/data`; functions `numbering_classify(home, dialled)` → category (`emergency`, `service`, `landline`, `mobile`, `national`, `shared_cost`, `toll_free`, `premium`, `international`, `invalid`), libphonenumber type, region, E.164, what to dial, emergency label; `numbering_route(extension, dialled)` and `asterisk.linx_route_outbound(endpoint, dialled)` add `allowed` + `reason`). Until trunks exist (steps 2–3) every call but an emergency one comes back `no_lines`; steps 2–3 add permission levels and lines to `numbering_route` (the return type changes then). Extension numbers: a trigger refuses numbers starting with the national or international prefix, emergency numbers and short service numbers (UAE: `0…`, 999/998/997/112/901, 4451/4456) with API error `number_reserved`; it checks only when a number is set, so a country change lists clashes (`store.ExtensionClashes`) rather than locking extensions. `store.SyncNumbering` runs at every control-plane start. Docker test `internal/store/numbering_docker_test.go` compares the SQL with the library on 30,632 numbers (every example number for every country written the ways people dial, near-misses, every number up to 4 digits, random digits after the prefixes that matter).
- *As built (step 3, 2026-09-26):* migration `0018_trunk_routing.sql`. `numbering_lines(caller, e164, region, dial)`: every enabled trunk with an outbound priority, in order, with the number in its dial format (`e164` `+971501234567`; `00_prefix` `00971501234567`; `local` `0501234567` at home and `00…` abroad; emergency and service numbers always as dialled) and the caller ID (the caller's DID on that trunk, else the trunk's main number, else the provider's default). `numbering_route` returns `allowed` when there's a line (`no_lines` otherwise; `emergency` either way). Asterisk asks `asterisk.linx_outbound(endpoint, dialled)` → reason, category, withhold, lines (`endpoint/number/caller_id/max_calls&…`; replaces step 1's `linx_route_outbound`) and `asterisk.linx_inbound(endpoint, dialled)` (the DID's extension, however the number is written; `''` if it rings nobody; no row if the trunk doesn't own it), both `SECURITY DEFINER`. `linx-outbound` tries the lines in order and moves to the next only on `CHANUNAVAIL`/`CONGESTION` (down, full, refused certificate, 503), never after an answer, busy or refusal; limits with `GROUP_COUNT` (2 outside calls per extension, each trunk's `max_calls`, inbound calls count too), none for emergency calls; the caller ID is set on the outgoing channel's connected line in a pre-dial handler (PJSIP sends that as `From`/`P-Asserted-Identity`), withheld when the level says so (never for emergency). Spoken refusals: not permitted "I'm sorry. That feature is not available on this line", no line "All circuits are busy now. Please try your call again later", limit "Simultaneous call limit reached". `call.ended` gains outcomes `not_permitted`, `no_lines`, `limit_reached`. `linx route test` lists the lines ("Goes out on "UCM" as 0501234567, showing +97142000100.").
- **`linx route test NUMBER --from EXT`** prints the decision in plain words ("Mobile number, allowed for Staff. Goes out on Line A as +971501234567; on Line B if Line A is down."). It runs the same SQL function, so it can't disagree with a real call. It's the engine of 1E's call simulator.

## 6. Encryption and certificates (ADR-023, ADR-045)
- The probe tries, in order: TLS with a publicly trusted certificate whose name matches the provider's address; TLS with **a certificate or CA the admin pins** (many LAN devices like the UCM6304 and some providers use their own); only then, with the ADR-023 confirmation, TCP or UDP without TLS. Media: SRTP (SDES over TLS) first; RTP without encryption only with the same confirmation. A trunk with TLS but unencrypted audio counts as unencrypted.
- **Pinning is verification, not an exception**: Linx checks the provider's certificate against exactly what the admin approved (the fingerprint is shown in plain words at `linx trunk add`, to compare with the provider's). Certificate checking is never switched off (security rules).
- Asterisk checks certificates per transport, not per trunk, so trunks use one TLS client transport whose CA list is the system's public CAs plus every pinned CA/certificate; the name is always checked (step 3 proves it with a wrong-name certificate).
- `linx doctor` and the trunk list show every unencrypted trunk (ADR-023). No plaintext trunk over WireGuard gets the warning (ADR-024).
- Trunk calls are decrypted in Asterisk to transcode and bridge (as 1C's browser calls already are). Documented; end-to-end encryption is for app-to-app and meetings.
- *As built (step 3, 2026-09-26):* TLS trunks use the phones' `transport-tls` itself (`verify_server=yes`, `ca_list_file` = `/etc/asterisk/trunk-ca.pem`), not a separate client transport: a second TLS transport would need a second listening port, and phones only ever connect in, which these settings don't touch. The entrypoint builds `trunk-ca.pem` from the image's public CAs plus every pinned certificate that parses (a broken one is left out: OpenSSL would refuse the whole file, and the phones' transport with it); OpenSSL reads it again for every new connection to a provider, so pins apply without a restart. The call suite proves an unknown CA ("The certificate is untrusted") and a pinned CA with the wrong name ("The server identity does not match…") are both refused. The API now refuses what Asterisk's config or a SIP URI can't hold (host: a DNS name or IPv4 address; login: letters, digits, `. _ ~ + = -`; password: printable ASCII, no spaces at the ends, `;` escaped when written; caller ID: digits; a pin must be PEM certificates), and the renderer checks again. Unencrypted (TCP/UDP) trunks get `transport-trunk-tcp`/`-udp` on port 5062 (never 5060, never published), only while such a trunk exists; their SIP address is the **resolved IP address**, because Asterisk decides at start which transports its DNS resolver may use and these appear later (a name would never resolve). `linx doctor` doesn't count those two transports as phone connections.

## 7. WireGuard (ADR-024, ADR-046)
- Profiles: import the provider's `.conf` or fill in the fields; the private key is sealed (ADR-030) and never shown again. Several profiles; each trunk's **Connection** is "Internet" or one profile.
- **Split tunnel only:** a profile carries only its trunks' addresses. `AllowedIPs = 0.0.0.0/0` (or `::/0`) is narrowed to those addresses, with a note.
- `linx-wireguard` (Go, `golang.zx2c4.com/wireguard/wgctrl` MIT + `github.com/vishvananda/netlink` Apache-2.0, kernel WireGuard from Ubuntu 24.04) **joins Asterisk's network namespace** (`network_mode: service:asterisk`) and holds `NET_ADMIN` there only: Asterisk itself gets no capability, and no other container's routes change. It reads rendered profiles from its own memory-only volume, applies them (interface, keys, peers, routes to trunk addresses only), writes each tunnel's last handshake back for the control plane, and re-applies within seconds if Asterisk restarts (the namespace is new then; step 5 proves the recovery).
- A tunnel with no handshake for 3 minutes marks its trunks down (alert "WireGuard tunnel <name> is down"). Needs the `wireguard` kernel module (setup checks and loads it).
- *As built (step 5, 2026-09-26):*
  - **Rendering** (`internal/wgconf`, shared by both sides; `internal/trunkconf.Renderer`): with the trunks, the control plane writes `wireguard.json` (every profile, keys opened, endpoint resolved to IPv4 and kept on DNS failure, `AllowedIPs` = exactly the addresses of the enabled trunks using it, keep-alive 25 s unless the profile sets one) into the tmpfs volume `wireguard` (uid/gid 65532, `0700`; only the control plane and the agent mount it). Every profile gets a tunnel, so its handshake shows before a line uses it. The imported file's `AllowedIPs`, `DNS` etc. aren't used; an `AllowedIPs = 0.0.0.0/0` (or any other) import returns a note (`notes` on the create response) saying only the lines' addresses go through. Profile create/change/delete re-renders at once. The profile's `address` must include an IPv4 address (added as a /32).
  - **A trunk through a tunnel must have an IPv4 host** (`host_must_be_address`): the tunnel carries only addresses known in advance; a name could resolve elsewhere, outside it. Such trunks get their own transports with **no address rewriting** (Asterisk otherwise puts the LAN address in Contact and SDP, and the provider would answer there): `transport-wg-udp`/`-tcp` on 5063 and `transport-wg-tls` on 5064 (from a `pjsip.conf` template holding the certificate settings), each in the trunk file only while a trunk needs it; nothing extra listens otherwise (the call suite checks Asterisk's listeners). Being dialled by address, a TLS trunk through a tunnel needs a certificate naming that address (or a pinned one). Verified: Via, Contact and `c=` carry the tunnel address, never the LAN one. No ADR-023 warning (plain UDP inside the tunnel is the CLI's default).
  - **Fail closed** (not in the design, found building it): a tunnel's trunks are rendered into their own file, `wg-<interface>.conf`, headed by the tunnel address and the trunks' addresses. Asterisk's entrypoint includes a tunnel's file (via `/etc/asterisk/pjsip_wireguard.conf`) only while the kernel sends to every one of those addresses **from the tunnel's address** (a connected UDP socket's source, no privilege needed), and reloads when that changes. So after an Asterisk restart, or if a tunnel vanishes, its lines are simply absent (outgoing calls fail over or hear "no lines") instead of their SIP leaving unencrypted by the normal route.
  - **`linx-wireguard`** (`services/wireguard`, image `linx-wireguard` from `go-service.Dockerfile`; compose service `wireguard`, `network_mode: service:asterisk`, read-only, `cap_drop: ALL`, `cap_add: NET_ADMIN, SETUID, SETGID`, no-new-privileges). Docker gives a non-root user no capabilities, so it starts as root only to re-run itself as uid 65532 with NET_ADMIN as its one ambient capability (SETUID/SETGID are gone after that switch). Every 2 s: applies each tunnel (interface `linx-wg<8 hex of the profile id's hash>`, MTU 1420, the one peer, address /32, a /32 route per allowed address with the tunnel address as source) only when it changed (reconfiguring can drop the session), removes `linx-wg*` interfaces no profile names, and keeps one policy rule: every packet **not** marked 0x4c58 looks up table 0x4c58 first, and the tunnels mark their own encrypted packets, so a provider whose SIP server shares its WireGuard endpoint's address still works. Addresses on Asterisk's own networks, or its tunnel address, are never routed into a tunnel (reported as skipped). Writes `wireguard-status.json` (per tunnel: when applied, last handshake, bytes, error; no keys) to the tmpfs volume `wireguard-status`. **Asterisk restarts:** the agent's namespace loses its network cards (veth), it exits, and `restart: unless-stopped` starts it in the new namespace (tested: the line is back within seconds).
  - **State and alert** (`trunk.Monitor`, migration `0020_wireguard_status.sql`): each profile's `status` (`up`, `connecting` until 3 minutes after it was applied, `down`, `unknown` while the agent isn't reporting), `status_detail`, `status_since`, `last_handshake_at` (API: read-only fields on `/wireguard-profiles`). A down tunnel makes its lines `unreachable` ("Its WireGuard tunnel … is down") and raises "WireGuard tunnel <name> is down" (warning, naming its lines) while a line uses it; resolved when it's up again, unused, or deleted.
  - **Probe:** for a line through a tunnel the control plane can't reach the provider (only Asterisk's namespace has the tunnel): `POST /trunks/{id}/test` reports the tunnel's state (step `tunnel`) and says the line's own state shows in its status.
  - **CLI:** `linx trunk wireguard add NAME < provider.conf` (the file comes in on standard input, never on the command line or screen; prints the note and Linx's public key), `list` (state, lines, public key), `remove`. `linx trunk add --wireguard NAME [--transport udp|tcp|tls] --host <address inside the tunnel>`; interactively it asks "internet, or a tunnel" when profiles exist.
  - **Doctor "Phone lines":** each tunnel's state (a down tunnel with lines fails), the `wireguard` kernel module (`/sys/module/wireguard`), and `linx-wireguard` running, when any profile exists; `transport-wg-*` on 5063/5064 aren't phone connections. **Setup** writes `/etc/modules-load.d/linx-wireguard.conf` and runs `modprobe wireguard`.
  - Tests: `internal/wgconf`, `services/wireguard` (fake kernel: apply-on-change, skipped addresses, stale interfaces, missing module, orphaned namespace), trunkconf/asteriskconf/monitor/doctor/CLI units, and `internal/calltest/wireguard_docker_test.go` (in `make test-calls`; needs `make image SERVICE=wireguard` and the host's module): a "provider" container (`internal/calltest/wgpeer`, netlink + wgctrl) with plain-UDP SIPp inside its tunnel end, the real agent image in Asterisk's namespace; import with `AllowedIPs = 0.0.0.0/0` narrowed, handshake, call out (tunnel address in Via/Contact/SDP), call in to a DID, tunnel removed → line leaves Asterisk and a call hears "no lines" with nothing sent, agent back → line back, Asterisk restarted → agent follows, line back.

## 8. Home networks and NAT
- Registration trunks over TLS: one outbound connection kept open; calls come in on it. Nothing to forward.
- **Audio from internet providers** goes straight between Asterisk and the provider over UDP (it's server-to-provider, so ADR-042's "443 only" is about people's devices, not this). Linx sends audio first and most providers answer to where it came from, so most homes need no port forward. When a test call shows one-way audio, setup's generated steps say which router forward to add (UDP 10000–10199 → the Linx server), and the host firewall admits it **only from trunk addresses**.
- IP-authenticated trunks need a stable address (warned at `linx trunk add`); the DNS follower (1C) keeps `sip.<domain>` current for providers that accept a name.
- The phones' 5061 and audio ports stay closed to everyone else on the internet, as since 1B.

## 9. Fraud, alerts, events
- **Built-in limits:** no trunk-to-trunk; outside calls only from signed-in devices and browser lines; at most 2 outside calls at once per extension and the trunk's own limit (Asterisk `GROUP_COUNT`); international and premium off by default; emergency always allowed and never limited.
- **Alerts** (existing engine): trunk down / back up (registration failing or unreachable for 2 minutes), WireGuard tunnel down, **emergency call placed** (who, when; so someone knows help was called), **unusual international calling** (more than 30 minutes or 10 calls to international numbers in an hour, both adjustable), and "a country called for the first time".
- **Webhooks:** `trunk.created/updated/deleted`, `trunk.status_changed` (registered, unreachable, …); call events gain `direction` (`internal`/`inbound`/`outbound`), `trunk_id`, and the outside number in E.164.
- **Audit:** every trunk, route, permission and country change; every unencrypted-trunk confirmation records who confirmed the warning.
- *As built (step 4, 2026-09-26):* **Trunk state doesn't come over ARI** (changed from the design; owner OK'd it: what matters is seeing it): ARI has no event or resource for outbound registrations. Asterisk's entrypoint runs `pjsip show registrations` and `pjsip show contacts` every 10 s and writes `trunk-status.json` into the memory-only volume `trunk-status` (uid 100, 0755, states only; `internal/trunkstatus`: parsers checked against the real image's output, `Decide`). The control plane's `trunk.Monitor` (every 10 s) turns it into each trunk's `status` (`registered`, `reachable`, `rejected` = answers but refuses the login, `unreachable`, `unknown`, `disabled`), `status_detail` and `status_since` (migration `0019_trunk_status.sql`; not a setting, so the etag doesn't change), fires `trunk.status_changed` in the same transaction, and raises "Phone line X is down" once it has been down **2 minutes** (`alert.Engine.FireAfter`: the engine's hold-back shortened from 5), critical when no other outgoing line works ("emergency calls included"), closed when it's back, turned off or deleted. A report older than a minute (Asterisk restarting) leaves every status as it was. **Call alerts** (`trunk.CallAlerts`, the call tracker's `CallWatcher`): every emergency number dialled is announced at once, whether a line carries it or not (`alert.Engine.Announce`: a one-shot alert, `alert.one_shot`, sent at the next tick and closed as it's sent, no "resolved" notice); a country called for the first time (table `called_region`) likewise; "unusual calling abroad" when the last hour's calls to international or premium numbers abroad, those in progress included (checked every minute), pass 30 minutes or 10 calls (`pbx_setting.international_alert_minutes/calls`, adjustable through `PUT /outbound-routing`'s `international_alert`), sent at once and resolved when the hour is back under both. Calls that went out are kept 30 days in `outside_call` for this count only (not call history). **Call events** gain `direction`, `trunk_id` and `outside_number` (E.164 when readable); an outside party is `{name, number}` in `from`/`answered_by` (`CallParty`'s `extension`/`device_id` became optional); `/calls/active` gains `direction`, `trunk_id`, `ringing`. The tracker decides direction the way the dialplan does (Go `numbering.Classify`, which the SQL is tested against), and learns an inbound call's DID from the dialplan: `linx-trunk-call` passes the call through `linx-trunk-did` with the DID as its extension (ari.conf's `channelvars` would have carried a variable instead, but crashed the amd64 build's Asterisk as the ARI app was created).

## 10. API and commands
- `/trunks` (CRUD, merge patch + `If-Match` like extensions), `POST /trunks/{id}/test` (the probe: DNS, TLS and certificate, OPTIONS, registration, SRTP acceptance; results in plain words), `/trunks/{id}/dids`, `/wireguard-profiles` (CRUD, `.conf` import), `/inbound-routes`, `/outbound-routing` (country, primary/backup, caller ID), `/call-permission-levels`, `POST /route-test`. Provider secrets are write-only (never returned).
- New scopes: `trunks:read`, `trunks:write` and `routing:write` (**sensitive**, never in "all": they can make calls that cost money), `routing:read`. Admin/system_admin only.
- CLI (root, `docker exec` like `linx user`): `linx trunk add|list|test|remove`, `linx route test`. `linx trunk add` is the guided form: template or generic → address → login → probe → ADR-023 warning if needed → DIDs → primary/backup.
- `linx doctor` "Phone lines" section: each trunk registered/reachable, its encryption (unencrypted ones listed), certificate expiry for pinned ones, WireGuard handshakes, firewall sets match IP-authenticated trunks, the country is set and emergency numbers route somewhere, no extension clashes with an emergency number.
- *As built (step 4, 2026-09-26):* **The probe** (`internal/trunkprobe`): address (IPv4, as Asterisk uses; refuses loopback, link-local, multicast and Linx's own container networks), connection, TLS certificate (public CAs plus the trunk's pin, name always checked, never switched off: a failed certificate is described from Go's `tls.CertificateVerificationError`, with its SHA-256 fingerprint), SIP `OPTIONS`, and for registration trunks the login, as a `REGISTER` **with no Contact** (a query: proves the password without changing where the provider sends calls; digest MD5 or SHA-256). Audio encryption isn't provable without placing a call: `ok` if the OPTIONS answer offers `RTP/SAVP`, otherwise "checked on the first call"; unencrypted lines also try TLS on 5061 and say so if it answers. Runs from the control plane (WireGuard lines: the tunnel's state, step 5). `POST /trunks/{id}/test` (`trunks:write`, returns every step in plain words, the presented certificates and `untrusted`), `POST /route-test` (`routing:read`, the same decision and words as `linx route test`). Trunks gain `status`, `status_detail`, `status_since`. **CLI** `linx trunk add|list|test|remove` (`cmd/linx/trunk.go` → `docker exec -i [-t]` → `services/control-plane/trunk_cmd.go`): `add` asks template → name → address → port → login and password (not echoed), tests, offers to pin the presented certificate after showing its fingerprint, offers (with the ADR-023 warning) to go unencrypted only if TLS fails, refuses to save a failing line unless confirmed, then phone numbers → extension and primary/backup/no for outgoing; every answer has a flag (`--yes` asks nothing and never pins or goes unencrypted by itself). It renders the trunks file itself, so Asterisk has the line within seconds. `list` shows status, security and outgoing order. **Doctor "Phone lines"** (`internal/doctor/lines.go`): the country, each line's state asked of Asterisk now (the database's copy if it can't be), unencrypted lines and who confirmed them, pinned certificates expiring within 30 days, emergency calls having a working outgoing line, extension clashes. WireGuard handshakes came with step 5 (§7); firewall sets come with step 6.
- *As built (step 2, 2026-09-26):* `/trunks` (+ nested `/trunks/{id}/dids` to create a DID, flat `/dids/{id}` to change or remove one, read-only `/inbound-routes` listing every DID across every trunk), `/wireguard-profiles` (`config` accepts a whole wg-quick file, or fill in the fields; the public key is derived from the private key, never asked for), `/call-permission-levels`, `/outbound-routing` (`GET`/`PUT`; primary/backup is purely the order `PUT` sets — no separate settings row). `POST /trunks/{id}/test` and `POST /route-test` wait for the probe (step 4). New package `internal/trunk` (migration `0017_trunks.sql`); `internal/pbx`'s `Extension` gained `call_permission_level_id`. `numbering_route` (migration 0016) now checks it: an extension with **no level assigned fails closed** (`not_permitted`, emergency only) exactly like one whose level excludes the category; an allowed category still comes back `no_lines` until step 3 gives it real lines. Provider template catalogue (§3) is `internal/trunk/templates.yaml`, read by step 4's `linx trunk add`, not its own endpoint. Scopes and events as designed above (webhook catalog: `trunk.created/updated/deleted/status_changed`, `status_changed` fires from step 4). ## 12. Security in this slice
- New outside surfaces: outbound connections to providers (TLS verified or pinned), and for IP-authenticated trunks 5061 (plus 5060 for ADR-023 trunks) open **only to the provider's addresses**, enforced twice (host firewall set and Asterisk's ACL). Registration trunks open nothing.
- Provider passwords and WireGuard private keys: sealed in the database (ADR-030), rendered only into memory-only volumes readable by the one container that needs them, never in views, API responses, events, audit details or logs.
- Toll fraud: §9 by construction (contexts), by limits and by alerts. `trunks:write`/`routing:write` sensitive.
- `linx-wireguard`: the only container with `NET_ADMIN`, only in Asterisk's namespace; non-root where the capability allows, read-only filesystem, no Docker socket.
- `linx-firewall-sync` runs as root on the host (it changes nftables), reads only address lists from the control plane through `docker exec` (the same trust as `linx user`), and can only add or remove addresses in Linx's own sets, never rules.
- Untrusted input from providers (caller names, numbers, SIP headers) sanitised before the dialplan, API, webhooks and web.
- THREAT_MODEL gets rows for trunks, the rendered-config volume, outbound routing, WireGuard, the firewall sync and toll fraud at the review step.
