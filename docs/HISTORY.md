Built: skeleton + tokens, CI, `linx setup` (prereqs, domain/DNS token, compose stack), `linx-certd` (Cloudflare + DuckDNS, wildcard default), step-ca bootstrap, `linx doctor` certificate checks.

  - Step 1 done: database (Postgres, control plane, migrations, encryption).
  - Step 2 done: API skeleton — `api/openapi.yaml`, `make api` codegen (oapi-codegen strict server, `services/control-plane/api/gen.go`), kin-openapi request validation, problem+json, cursor pagination, `/me`, `/openapi.json`, `/event-types`.
  - Step 3 done: authentication — `internal/auth` (keys, scopes/roles, EdDSA tokens, rate limits, middleware, `/oauth/token`), `internal/store` (Postgres), migration 0002, `/api-keys` + `/oauth-clients` endpoints, `linx api-key create|list|revoke`, `linx_jwt_signing_key` secret. Scopes are declared per operation in `openapi.yaml` (`security: [bearer: [...]]`) and enforced by the validator.
  - Step 4 done: webhooks — `internal/safehttp` (SSRF guard, shared by alert senders), `internal/webhook` (Standard Webhooks signer, sender, outbox worker, service), `internal/store/webhooks.go`, migration 0003, `/webhooks*`, `/webhook-deliveries/*`, `/outbound-allowlist`. Emit events with `webhook.NewEvent` + `store.InsertEvent` in the change's transaction.   - Step 5 done: admin alerts — `internal/alert` (channel config + 6 senders: ntfy, Gotify, Slack, Teams, Telegram, generic webhook; service; engine), `internal/store/alerts.go`, migration 0004, `/alert-channels*`, `/alerts`. `Fire`/`Resolve` are cheap in-process calls (see `services/control-plane/main.go`'s `webhookDisabledKey` and `certdpoll.go`); the engine's tick decides notify/remind/resolve timing, severity and quiet hours. Sources wired: webhook disabled, certificate renewal (polls `linx-certd`'s `/metrics` on `linx-private`, not through the SSRF guard — that's for admin URLs, not Linx's own services). Disk/storage and DDNS deferred: no filesystem/DDNS signal reaches the control plane yet (`docs/THREAT_MODEL.md`).

## Pre-launch: backup and restore (2026-09-27)

A backup is always two files, paired by a tag so they can never be mixed: a `pg_dump` of the database, and the two Docker secrets a restore can't regenerate (`linx_db_encryption_key`, `linx_jwt_signing_key`). Restore is deliberately two steps, not the one the owner first asked for entirely in the web wizard: the keys step (`linx restore-secrets`) has to be a host/CLI operation, since Docker secrets are files the control plane's own container is deliberately never given a way to write (the very boundary the security audit above just confirmed is being enforced) — the database step still happens in the browser, as the new first screen of the setup wizard ("Set up fresh" or "Restore from a backup"), since the control plane already talks to its own database directly and needs no new privilege for that part. - **Phase 2 step 6 — the app rings (2026-10-04, `docs/PHASE2.md` §12 step 6 and "Step 6, as built", ADR-074 and the new ADR-078):** **a sleeping iPhone or iPad rings now.** The rule the whole step is shaped by is Apple's: a VoIP push must report a call to CallKit *at once, every time*, or iOS kills the app and stops delivering its pushes (§14 item 1). **Ready before there is a screen** (`ios/Linx/App/AppDelegate.swift`): PushKit only delivers to an app that was already listening, and a push to a closed app launches it with no window, so registering happens in the delegate's first moment and `AppModel.shared` is the one model the delegate and the screens share. **The order of things** (`AppModel.woken`, `PhoneModel.woken`): the system is told there is a call, with the caller's number from the push → Apple's completion handler is called → *then* the app signs in, asks for its phone line and registers, while the server holds the call. A phone woken for a call that never arrives stops ringing after 20 seconds and **closes its line again**, because a registration left behind would make Linx think the phone is awake and skip the push next time. **The system owns the call** (`ios/Linx/Core/Call`): every button goes one way round — the app *asks* the system (`CXCallController`), the system *tells* the app (`CXProviderDelegate`), and only then does the app act, so the lock screen, a car, a headset and the app's own screen can never disagree; outgoing calls are reported too, so they show in Recents. `SystemCalls` is the seam (`CallKitCalls`, and `NoSystemCalls` for China, the screenshot harness and the tests — which is also what keeps a test run off the system's call services). **The sound waits to be handed over:** WebRTC in **manual-audio** mode whenever CallKit is in charge, enabled only in `provider(_:didActivate:)` (§14, "Audio path"). **Which call is which:** a push carries the call's id but Asterisk's invitation doesn't, so they are matched on the caller's number (digits only) and otherwise on the longest wait; the phone has one line, so a second caller hears busy and goes to voicemail, and their push is reported and ended at once. **China (ADR-078, new):** Apple doesn't allow CallKit there, so a phone whose region is `CN` sends **no PushKit token at all** and tells Linx `call_alerts` instead — a ringing call arrives as a **time-sensitive notification** to tap, the app rings on its own screen, and the signed-in screen says so once (it moves to Settings in step 7). **Push tokens** (`POST /api/v1/me/phone-push`): the PushKit one, the notification one once the person allows notifications (asked once, when the phone has just been set up, never from the background), `call_alerts`, and **which Apple the build belongs to, read out of the app's own provisioning profile** (`PushEnvironment`), so a TestFlight phone and an Xcode build both ring with no setting. **What the app asks iOS for:** background modes **`voip` and `audio` and nothing else** (`ios/Info.plist`, the first keys Xcode has no build setting for) and the Push Notifications entitlement (`ios/Linx.entitlements`). **Server, small additions:** migration 0043 adds `device_identity.call_alerts` and widens `asterisk.linx_wake` to wait for a phone that can only be told by notification; the gateway sends it an alert push (`interruption-level: time-sensitive`, expiring with the ringing) carrying the same three facts and nothing else, counted as `linx_push_sent_total{kind="call"}`; `/me/phone-push` refuses `call_alerts` together with a VoIP token. Tests: `ios/LinxTests/RingingTests.swift` (62 app tests in all) and the Go side for the China path and `linx_wake`. The app **13760 → 13964 KB**, all of it Linx's own Swift, and the control plane **+0.01 MB** (`docs/RESOURCES.md`); `docs/THREAT_MODEL.md` "Push review" gained step 6's rows. **Still to prove on real devices at the demo** (§13): the lock screen, a force-quit app, Low Power Mode, overnight, a bad network, and push → ringing under two seconds. **Next: step 7**, the rest of the app — 1:1 video, Calls, Team with presence, Voicemail, Settings.
- **Phase 2 step 7 — the rest of the app, and 1:1 video (2026-10-04, `docs/PHASE2.md` §12 step 7 and "Step 7, as built", the new ADR-079):** the app is the whole app now — four tabs, **Calls**, **Team**, **Keypad** and **More** (Voicemail, Settings) — and a call can have a picture in it. Four tabs and no more: Meetings arrive with Phase 3 and Chat with Phase 4, and App Review refuses a tab that does nothing (§14 item 2). **Video is added to a call, never rung as one (ADR-079, new):** a Linx call rings as an ordinary call, which is what the lock screen, CarPlay and a headset understand, and either side then puts a camera into it with a **re-INVITE** (`SIPUserAgent.setVideo`, `Core/Media/Camera.swift`, the picture in `WebRTCMedia`); turning it off is another one, and the conversation stops for neither. A side that won't have it gets a `rollback` (RFC 8829) and the call carries on untouched; 491 (both at once) and a mid-call challenge are handled the same way, each with its own ACK on the re-INVITE's own branch and sequence number. The **video button** in the Team list rings first and turns the camera on the moment they answer. **A camera is only ever switched on by the person holding the phone:** an offer that adds video is answered `recvonly`, so their picture appears and nothing of this phone's is sent until its own button is pressed. **Small on purpose** (the low-bandwidth rule): H.264 first (an iPhone does it in hardware, which the battery notices), 640×480 at 24 frames, **600 kbit/s** written into the SDP (`b=AS`) and the sender's settings, and when WebRTC reports under 150 kbit/s to spare for three readings running — a quarter of a minute — **the camera goes off by itself** and the person is told the call goes on. **The screen follows the screen, not the device** (`CallLayout`, the owner's ask this day): a phone upright (picture full-bleed, buttons along the bottom), a phone on its side (buttons in a column down the trailing edge, clear of the picture and the camera), and **two panels** on an iPad and on an **iPhone Duo opened out** — side by side when the screen is square-ish, so nothing anyone presses lands on the crease, over-and-under on a tall iPad; the Duo's outer screen is a narrow phone and gets the first layout. It is one pure function with tests at the real device sizes, and step 8 swaps the near-square rule for the fold's own geometry (`ReservedRegion`, `ArrangementView`, iOS 27.1) without touching anything else. **Server, two small things:** migration 0044 gives the app's and the browser's endpoints `h264,vp8` and one video stream (a desk phone or gateway keeps exactly the codecs it had), and Asterisk passes the picture between the two WebRTC endpoints without transcoding it; and `GET /api/v1/team/live` now takes a **device token** in the `Authorization` header with no `Origin`, exactly as `/sip` does, closing when the phone is stopped. Everything else the screens read — the team, call history, voicemail, the two badges, "my status" — are the endpoints the web client already uses, with a phone's ordinary read-only scopes. **The tabs:** Calls (history, missed in red, All/Missed, ring back, older pages; opening it clears the badge for that *person* on every device), Team (live over the websocket, presence dots with the words beside them, search, my own status, call or video-call anyone), Keypad (with a **Video** button in a call) and More → Voicemail (play, which marks it heard, swipe to delete, ring back; the audio is fetched for one message at a time, played out of memory and never written to the phone) and Settings (status, the line, the camera, how calls arrive here — the China note moved here from the signed-in screen — and **Sign out of this phone**). Still nothing in the background: the Team websocket lives only while the app is in front, nothing polls, and a badge is fetched only when Linx says its counter moved, so one person's numbers can never reach another's phone. Tests: `ios/LinxTests/VideoTests.swift`, `CallLayoutTests` and `ScreensDataTests` (87 app tests in all), `TestTeamLiveForAPhone` on the Go side, and migration 0044's row in `internal/db`. The app **13 → 15 MB** (WebRTC unchanged at 12 MB; Linx's own program 1.9 → 3.1 MB for the new screens, SwiftUI's generated code being most of it) and a video call costs about **4.5 MB a minute** each way at the ceiling against 0.2 MB for sound alone (`docs/RESOURCES.md`). Screenshots on an iPhone (upright and on its side) and an iPad, light and dark; the simulator has no camera, so both video tiles show their "camera off" state. **Needs the owner's look**, and the iPhone Duo shots want the Xcode 27.1 beta. **Next: step 8**, the fold layouts and the Duo screenshots.

- **Row 2.4 on a real iPhone: a force-quit app didn't ring (2026-10-04, fixed).** The first failure found by `docs/TEST_MATRIX.md`, and it was in the Asterisk image, not in the app. The wake step asked `${DEVICE_STATE(PJSIP/…)}` whether each app phone was already connected and used `${IF(…)}` to build the list to wake — and **neither function existed in the image**: `deploy/docker/asterisk.Dockerfile`'s menuselect step disables every category and names the modules back one by one, and `func_devstate` and `func_logic` were never named. Asterisk logged `Function DEVICE_STATE not registered` once a call and carried on with an empty answer, which the dialplan read as "the phone is already here", so `WAKELIST` stayed empty, no `UserEvent(LinxWake, …)` was ever sent, no push ever reached Apple, and the call went straight to voicemail (the owner's phone, with a good production push token on file and the Apple key in place, never rang). Rows 2.1 to 2.3 passed throughout because an app that is still running holds its own SIP line and needs no push at all. Three changes: both modules are now built (**+146 KB**, `docs/RESOURCES.md`); the dialplan treats **only a state Asterisk actually answered** as "already here", so an empty answer wakes the phone instead of skipping it (one redundant push is harmless — the app ties it to the invitation that follows — while the other way round is a phone that never rings); and the call suite gained the test that would have caught it, **"every dialplan function is in the image"**, which reads every `${NAME(…)}` out of the rendered `extensions.conf` and `func_odbc.conf` and asks the running Asterisk for each one (`core show function`), plus **"a sleeping app phone is woken"**, an end-to-end case with an `ios` device that has a push token and never signs in: the wake event must name that phone and the caller, and no call waits when no Apple key is in place. Checked on the old image first, where the new check names exactly `DEVICE_STATE` and `IF`.
- **Four things the owner found on a real iPhone, all fixed the same day (2026-10-04).** None of them could have been seen on a simulator. (1) **The loudspeaker did nothing.** The button asked the audio session once, but the system sets that session up again under the app every time CallKit hands a call over and at every route change, and each of those put the sound back in the earpiece. What the person asked for is now remembered and applied again at each of those moments, with the category set alongside the override (an override on its own is dropped by the next reconfiguration), and a headset or car arriving beats the button — the button then follows the sound rather than the other way round (`WebRTCMedia`, a new `onSpeakerChanged` through `SIPCallMedia` to `PhoneModel`). (2) **A call ringing while the app was open showed twice**: the app's own full-screen call and CallKit's banner at the same time. The ring belongs to the system — its banner over the app, or the lock screen — so the app now waits and shows its call screen once the call has been answered (`PhoneModel.showsCallScreen`); where CallKit may not be used (ADR-078) the app's screen is still the only ring there is, which is also what the `incoming-call` screenshot now shows. (3) **The keypad didn't use the screen**: it sat at the top of an iPhone 18 Pro Max with a third of the screen empty under it. It is now laid out like the iPhone's own Phone app (owner's words): keys as big as the width allows (68–112 pt, scaled to the screen), a tenth of the width as a margin down each side, the number just above the keys, and the whole block standing in the middle of the screen. - **Three more from the owner's phone (2026-10-04): which way the app turns, who counts as reachable, and Recents.** (1) **Every page turned with the phone.** A keypad or a list of people lying on its side is nobody's idea of a phone, and the iPhone's own Phone app doesn't turn at all. (2) **"A phone is not a browser" (owner's words).** The Team list called a person offline the moment their app stopped running — which on an iPhone is seconds after it goes in a pocket, because iOS suspends it — although ringing them wakes the phone in about two seconds. (3) **Turning "Show Linx calls in the Phone app" off didn't stop them appearing.** The setting was handed to the live CallKit provider, and iOS reads `includesCallsInRecents` when the provider is **made**: a new configuration on an existing one changes nothing. - **Build 2 killed the sound, and what that taught us (2026-10-04).** The owner's first report on build 2: the Phone app's Recents really was clean now, but **the echo test was silent, and an outgoing call over 5G connected with no sound either way**. The cause was the loudspeaker fix from earlier the same day: it set the audio session's **category** again — and did so from `didActivate`, the moment CallKit hands the session over and WebRTC starts its audio unit. Reconfiguring the session at that instant stops the sound dead, both directions, on a real iPhone (a simulator never shows it: there is no CallKit audio session to reconfigure). **The rule now written into the code: while CallKit has the session, the category and the mode are never touched.** `setSpeaker` moves the **output** and nothing else, and the route watcher only *reads* the route — it never puts one back by force, because fighting the system for a route is the other way to end up silent. Two things came out of it worth keeping: the loudspeaker button now shows **where the sound actually is** ("Earpiece", "Speaker", a headset's name), read from the system each time it moves rather than assumed from what was asked, and if the system refuses to move it the person is told instead of being left with a lit button and no sound. The same report also found **Stop video didn't work**: the camera had gone on in a call whose far side never answered the change, and every later press did nothing, so the phone sat on the video screen with no way back. Stopping is now unconditional — the camera is the person's, it goes off whatever is still in the air, and only the message telling the other side waits its turn (one change at a time, RFC 3261 §14.1). Tested: the far side never answering is now a case in `VideoTests`.
- **Build 3 on the phone: the sound was back, and four more things (2026-10-04).** Test my sound worked, the loudspeaker worked — **on Wi-Fi**. On 5G the call connected, the far end rang, and neither side heard anything, which also made the loudspeaker look broken there: one fault, not two. Checked from outside the owner's network (a STUN binding request from the test VPS to `turn.home.mym.ae:443`): the relay answers on **both** UDP and TLS, so the server end was sound and the fault was the app's. Two things in it, both real: (1) **the app never refreshed the relay's credentials.** They last an hour (`internal/turn`, TTL), a phone is signed in for days, and the browser has refreshed them five minutes before expiry since Phase 1C — the app took them once with its line and kept them. On Wi-Fi a call can often find another way through; on a mobile network the relay **is** the way, so the call connects and nobody hears anything. The app now refreshes them on the same schedule and hands them to a call already up (`PhoneModel.keepTheRelayFresh`, `SIPCallMedia.use(relay:)` → `RTCPeerConnection.setConfiguration`). (2) **the offer waited only 3 seconds for routes.** Enough on Wi-Fi, where the phone has one in milliseconds; not enough on a mobile link, where every route goes through the relay — a TLS handshake and an allocation on a radio that has just woken. Now 10 seconds, and the offer still leaves the moment a relay route is in hand. Also fixed from the same report: **the sound button is the Phone app's** (owner: "why don't you use a similar behaviour of the phone app?") — a plain Speaker switch while it is the phone's own earpiece or loudspeaker, and the **system's own route picker** (`AVRoutePickerView`), wearing the device's name, once a headset or a car is connected, which also means iOS moves the sound rather than Linx asking it to; the diagnostic "Earpiece/Speaker" label is gone. **The screen goes dark at an ear** (proximity, and only on the phone's own earpiece: not on the loudspeaker, not with a headset, never during video). And **their picture is only on screen while pictures are arriving** — the echo test stops echoing the moment this phone's camera goes off, which is why the big picture froze; a count of frames decoded that stops moving now takes it away.
